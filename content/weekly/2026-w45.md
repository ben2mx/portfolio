**November 2 to 8, 2026**

## What shipped

- **Gave our SASE platform its first full tune-up since rollout, across about 20 sites.** Getting it live came first; this week was set aside to go back through it. I went through every console setting the API cannot see and turned what I found into a 31-item list of things to redesign, rethink or fix, now tracked as real work.
- **Moved bandwidth to where it is actually used, without buying any.** I pulled 60 days of hourly usage per site, separated sites that run local backups from regular offices, and shifted licensed bandwidth from idle offices to the three sites that kept hitting their cap: +46%, +60% and +31%. Same license pool, zero downtime.
- **Cut the encrypted-traffic inspection bypass list by 44%**, from 59 entries to 33, after checking each one against what the vendor already exempts. Fewer bypasses means more traffic gets inspected.
- **Tightened the firewall rules between sites.** Turned two legacy-protocol blocks back on, narrowed a scanner exception to the one machine that uses it, and retired old per-person exceptions: disable first, delete after 30 days if nothing breaks.
- **Restored malware-domain protection for a group of machines that had quietly lost it.** An exception meant for one lab machine had ended up applying to everything behind the same DNS server. I pointed the lab machine at its own resolver, removed the broad exception, and confirmed the lab still updates. The lesson: in a cloud firewall, the "source" is whoever forwards the traffic, not whoever asked for it.
- **Fixed the reason the VPN seat pool kept filling up.** Offboarding disabled accounts but never removed their VPN access. I checked every seat holder against the directory, found about 20 seats to reclaim, and updated the offboarding checklist so it stops happening.
- **Gave a partner a fixed address to allow-list** by pinning one site's traffic to a dedicated public exit, without changing anything else for that site.
- **Wrote it down.** Two knowledge-base articles (which rule to change for which kind of block, and how to unblock a website) and a refreshed map of the environment.

## What it means

**For the company:** busy sites get the bandwidth they need from the licenses we already pay for, and more traffic is inspected for threats.

**For the IT team:** the platform is understood, documented and on a tracked plan, so routine blocks and unblocks no longer depend on one person knowing where to look.
